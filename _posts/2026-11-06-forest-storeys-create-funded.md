Excited to share that our project *Forest Storeys: Where Spider Webs Occur, What They Capture, and What That Reveals About Forest Structure* has been funded through the New Hampshire Agricultural Experiment Station's Collaborative Research Enhancement and Team Exploration (CREATE) program, starting November 2026.

This two-year project pairs our ongoing [spider-web eDNA work](/posts/2026/6/spiderweb-edna-hatch-funded/) with LiDAR-derived measures of forest structure, asking how canopy architecture, understory density, and other structural features shape where spider webs form and what genetic material they capture. The goal is to use forest structure itself to help predict and interpret eDNA biodiversity signals, adding a new structural dimension to our forest monitoring toolkit.

It's a great collaboration with [Remington Moll](https://colsa.unh.edu/person/rem-moll) and [Michael Palace](https://eos.unh.edu/person/michael-palace), director of UNH's Earth Systems Research Center and an expert in terrestrial and airborne LiDAR. More to come as we get sampling underway.
